# GTA 6 clip grammar

Operational rules for GTA 6 sources. Derived from 20 viral GTA 6 streamer clips published 17–23 Nov 2026 (see `C:\Users\cubei\NicheMachine\reference\gta6-viral-20\`). Clip IDs below are evidence, not required context. For GTA 6, direct visual and audio evidence from the MiMo pass leads candidate selection and ranking; transcript cues only support context and wording, and must be checked against the recording.

## What is different about GTA 6 footage

The game generates the incident; the streamer's reaction is the product. A clip needs both: the thing that happened and the face/voice reacting to it. New-game discovery is itself a hook — the first alligator, the first giant crab, the first severed arm, the first celebrity cameo. Mission cutscenes are cinematic enough to clip as spectacle, and in-game radio/TV is a joke delivery system independent of gameplay.

## Candidate leads

Detect these in the transcript and the MiMo pass. Each has a start rule and an end rule.

1. **Recognition / self-reference** — NPC lookalike, celebrity cameo, trailer scene recreated. Cues: "that's me", "looks like me", a real name (Charlamagne), "this is the scene from the trailer". Start 2–3 s before the comparison lands; end after the denial or laugh. Evidence: C01, C16, C17.
2. **Creature encounter** — alligator, crab, shark, snake. Cues: scream, "oh my god", "get off me", weapon wheel opening, "don't eat me". Start on the approach, not the swim; keep the attack and the kill/escape; end on the post-fight line ("you gotta be careful out here in Florida"). Evidence: C08, C11, C13, C14, C19.
3. **Emergent NPC behavior** — fights, surrender, road rage, absurd dialogue. Cues: "are they fighting?", "did he just say I'm done?", laughter after a beat of silence. Start on the first line that names the behavior; keep the NPC's punchline and the streamer's disbelief; end on the last laugh. Evidence: C04, C07, C12.
4. **Fail with consequence** — car destroyed, save overwritten, death screen. Cues: "I'm cooked", "no way", "my car got destroyed", desk slam, "wasted". Start on the commitment ("ain't making it", the confirm prompt); keep the consequence and the reaction; end on the damage report. Evidence: C06, C18.
5. **Rage / setup damage** — desk slam, screaming at a friend, a repeated phrase. Cues: impact sound, "I'm not fucking playing", the same sentence three times. Start 2 s before the impact; keep the argument that caused it; end on the punchline repetition. Evidence: C05, C06.
6. **Absurd mission / cutscene** — plane fight, gore, ridiculous set piece. Start on the action, not the mission briefing; keep the full beat; end when the cutscene changes location. Evidence: C03, C10, C13.
7. **Milestone / launch emotion** — subscriber count, longest stream, "13 years". Start on the number or the yell; keep the gratitude and the context line; end before the return to gameplay. Evidence: C02, C09.
8. **Opinion / rant** — thesis in the first 5 s, comparison anchor, repeated claim, punchline analogy. Start on the thesis sentence; keep the strongest comparison and the final analogy; end on the punchline. Evidence: C15, C13.
9. **Radio / TV comedy** — in-game broadcast carrying the joke. Start on the host's line, not the driving; keep the streamer's laugh; end when he leaves the car or changes station. Evidence: C04, C16.
10. **Physics accident** — the player picks up or throws an object and it hits an NPC, who retaliates. Cues: "did I just", "oh shit", "lady, I'm sorry". Start on the pickup, keep the throw, the hit and the apology, end when the retaliation peaks. Evidence: C20.

## Cut rules

- **Start on the inciting object or line.** 15 of 20 clips open within 3 s on the NPC face, the animal, the crash, the save menu, the thesis, or the first shout. The five that open on a drive or menu (C03, C04, C05, C08) are the ones with dead lead-ins.
- **The reaction is the payoff.** Never end on the event. End on the last laugh, the last denial, or the damage report. Cutting at the moment of impact loses the clip.
- **Cut the search.** After a jumpscare, the streamer often roams for 60 s looking for the creature (C08). Keep the scare, the scream and the first "where did it go"; drop the search.
- **Keep the setup line that makes the payoff legible.** "Ain't making it" before the bridge; "I'm not starting from zero" before the save menu; "there's supposed to be music" before the silent trailer. One line, not a scene.
- **Do not crop out the facecam.** It is the reaction. Group clips keep all cams (C03, C14).
- **No text-card opening.** Start on gameplay or the face.

## Duration

Observed: median 47 s, most clips 38–55 s. Reaction and discovery 38–55 s; fail/stakes 43–78 s; rant 45–115 s.

- Default target for GTA 6: **40–55 s**, max 60 s.
- Rant: keep 45–60 s around the thesis and the strongest analogy; the full 114 s version only works on the VOD.
- Do not pad a 20 s reaction to hit 30 s. The data has no 20 s clips because the reaction needs room.

## Hooks and titles

Observed title shapes:

- `STREAMER [verb] [absurd object] in GTA 6` — "Gets ATTACKED By A GIANT CRAB", "THROWS A Girl Out Of A PLANE".
- `STREAMER [verb]` — "Finds His LOOKALIKE", "BREAKS HIS SETUP", "HITS 63 MILLION SUBSCRIBERS".
- Question / curiosity gap — "Literally WHAT is GOING ON in GTA 6??", "Wait, You Can DO THAT?".
- First-person claim — "He Thought The Car Would Make It", "Will He Make It Across?".

Rules: all caps for the verb phrase; the object is concrete (crab, save, alligator, trailer); no colon-plus-explanation; no "insane" or "crazy" without the object.

## Ranking priorities for the council

1. Direct audiovisual evidence and reaction authenticity — prioritize the visible event and audible delivery, such as a real scream, laugh, desk slam or meaningful silence. A transcript description alone cannot establish the moment.
2. Event novelty — first-time discovery language ("wait", "I didn't know", "you can do that?") outranks routine gameplay.
3. Standalone clarity — a viewer who never played GTA 6 must understand the stakes from the clip alone. If it needs the stream title, pass or extend the setup.
4. Payoff presence — the clip must contain the consequence, not just the setup.
5. Facecam visibility at the peak.

Penalize: lead-in driving, menu scrolling, searching after the scare, reaction cut off before it lands, clips that need the previous 5 minutes.

## Vocabulary to detect

`wasted`, `Lester`, `Mr. Krabs`, `Florida`, `Leonida`, `Vice City`, `Lucia`, `Jason`, `trailer`, `subscribers`, `save`, `alligator`, `crab`, `gator`, `strip club`, `radio`.

Some are GTA 6-specific (Leonida, Lucia, Jason), some are legacy GTA references used as jokes (Lester), some are real-world (Florida). A legacy reference in a new game is a recognition beat: the streamer expects the old system and the game does not have it (C05).
