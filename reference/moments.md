# Picking moments from a play session

A moment is anything you'd send a friend: something funny in a mission, a crash nobody planned, a cutscene line, a chase
that goes wrong, a glitch. BullyZone (over 620 million views) is built on scenes from GTA games.

## What the 🔥 marks mean
Each line of a chunk file has a mark from the session's own hype curve: how loud it got against the player's normal, how
wild the picture got, and laughs or shouts heard. 🔥🔥 = top 3 % of that session, 🔥 = top 8 %. A mark is a reason to look,
not a reason to keep.

## Keep it if
- It has enough context to understand who is doing what and why, followed by a clear payoff.
- It starts shortly before that setup and ends just after the payoff (the reaction, explosion, or result).
- Or it is a standalone moment that is immediately compelling without setup: clearly funny, surprising, impressive, exciting, angry, a genuine shout/laugh, or another unmistakable reaction.
- A context-free moment must earn its place on its own in the first seconds; ordinary loud speech, a generic greeting, background music, or a high hype score alone is not enough.
- It ends on the payoff. Cut right after it.

## Drop it if
- It has neither understandable setup/payoff nor a compelling standalone reaction.
- It needs the last five minutes to make sense.
- The game radio or a song is playing under it (claims and mutes).
- It's a menu, a loading screen, or the player reading something.

## Check context before cutting

The scanner's peak is a place to inspect, not a clip boundary or an automatic keep. Read the transcript around it and look at the footage before and after it. Extend the start to include the relevant setup; if the setup is still unclear, drop the candidate unless the standalone exception above applies. Do not pad the list to reach a target number.

## The window
Start 3–6 seconds before the setup, end 1–2 seconds after the payoff. 15–45 seconds. Look at the window with
`nm look` before you keep it.

## The hook (on screen for the first 3 seconds)
5–9 words, what's about to happen, not what happened: "He thought the cops were gone". Never spoil the payoff.
